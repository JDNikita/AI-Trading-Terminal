# Bullish and Bearish Engulfing Patterns

## Introduction

Engulfing patterns are a type of reversal pattern in technical analysis that can be used to identify potential trading opportunities. These patterns are characterized by a significant price movement followed by a rapid price reversal. In this section, we will explore the geometric definitions, order book microstructure, volume profile, and predicted price path of Bullish and Bearish Engulfing patterns.

## Geometric Definitions

A Bullish Engulfing pattern consists of two candlesticks:

1. A small bearish candlestick (B1) that closes below the midpoint of the previous candlestick.
2. A large bullish candlestick (B2) that opens above the midpoint of the previous candlestick and closes above the midpoint of B1.

The geometric definition of a Bullish Engulfing pattern can be expressed as:

$$\begin{aligned}
B1: &amp; \text{Close}_1 < \frac{\text{Open}_1 + \text{High}_1}{2} \\
B2: &amp; \text{Open}_2 > \frac{\text{Open}_1 + \text{High}_1}{2} \text{ and } \text{Close}_2 > \frac{\text{Open}_1 + \text{High}_1}{2}
\end{aligned}$$

Similarly, a Bearish Engulfing pattern consists of two candlesticks:

1. A small bullish candlestick (B1) that closes above the midpoint of the previous candlestick.
2. A large bearish candlestick (B2) that opens below the midpoint of the previous candlestick and closes below the midpoint of B1.

The geometric definition of a Bearish Engulfing pattern can be expressed as:

## Order Book Microstructure

The order book microstructure plays a crucial role in understanding the behavior of Engulfing patterns. When a Bullish Engulfing pattern forms, the order book typically undergoes the following changes:

1. **Liquidity Sweep**: As the price starts to move in the direction of the engulfing candle, the order book begins to sweep, with more liquidity being absorbed on the side of the price movement.
2. **Stop-Hunts**: As the price approaches the midpoint of the previous candlestick, stop-loss orders are triggered, causing the price to bounce off the stop-loss level. This is known as a stop-hunt.
3. **Limit Order Absorption**: As the price approaches the midpoint of the previous candlestick, limit orders are absorbed, causing the price to slow down and eventually reverse direction.

**Rejection Wicks: Hammers, Shooting Stars, Pinbars**
=====================================================

Rejection wicks, also known as hammer, shooting star, and pinbar patterns, are a type of chart pattern that can be used to identify potential trading opportunities. These patterns occur when a security's price is rejected by a support or resistance level, resulting in a wick or tail that extends beyond the main body of the candle.

### Hammer

A hammer is a bullish reversal pattern that forms when a security's price is rejected by a support level. The pattern consists of a small body with a long lower wick and a short upper wick.

**Structural Stop-Hunts**
------------------------

Hammer wicks can be used as structural stop-hunts, where the stop-loss is placed below the low of the hammer's lower wick, approximately 2.5x to 3x the body height. This allows the trader to capture a potential rebound in the security's price.

**Example:**

* Security: XYZ Stock
* Hammer Pattern: 10:00 AM - 10:30 AM
* Lower Wick: 10:20 AM - 10:30 AM (2.5x body height)
* Stop-Loss: 10:20 AM - 10:30 AM (2.5x body height below the low)

### Shooting Star

A shooting star is a bearish reversal pattern that forms when a security's price is rejected by a resistance level. The pattern consists of a small body with a long upper wick and a short lower wick.

**Structural Stop-Hunts**
------------------------

Shooting star wicks can be used as structural stop-hunts, where the stop-loss is placed above the high of the shooting star's upper wick, approximately 2.5x to 3x the body height. This allows the trader to capture a potential decline in the security's price.

**Example:**

* Security: ABC Stock
* Shooting Star Pattern: 10:00 AM - 10:30 AM
* Upper Wick: 10:20 AM - 10:30 AM (2.5x body height)
* Stop-Loss: 10:20 AM - 10:30 AM (2.5x body height above the high)

### Pinbar

A pinbar is a neutral pattern that forms when a security's price is rejected by a support or resistance level, resulting in a small body with a long upper or lower wick.

**Structural Stop-Hunts**
------------------------

Pinbar wicks can be used as structural stop-hunts, where the stop-loss is placed at the opposite end of the pinbar's wick, approximately 2.5x to 3x the body height. This allows the trader to capture a potential continuation in the security's price.

**Example:**

* Security: DEF Stock
* Pinbar Pattern: 10:00 AM - 10:30 AM
* Upper Wick: 10:20 AM - 10:30 AM (2.5x body height)
* Stop-Loss: 10:20 AM - 10:30 AM (2.5x body height above the high)

* **Hard Stop Execution Rules**: Sell when the security's price reaches the stop-loss level, and the wick is above the body height (2.5x to 3x)
